# Haven City - Living City and Threat Design

## References reviewed

- Cities: Skylines: daily citizen routines make traffic, health, employment and service failures visible instead of presenting them only as numbers.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities:_Skylines
- Frostpunk: crises are structured as preparation, escalation, sacrifice and recovery, with pressure on both resources and public morale.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frostpunk
- RimWorld: incidents form a story generator; difficulty comes from the sequence and interaction of events rather than isolated random damage.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RimWorld
- They Are Billions: expansion creates a larger perimeter to defend, while infection can cascade when weak districts are not protected.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/They_Are_Billions
- Into the Breach: dangerous actions are telegraphed before impact so the player can make an informed counter-move.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Into_the_Breach
- Surviving Mars: life-support redundancy and specialized infrastructure make preparation part of city planning.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surviving_Mars
- Project Zomboid: layered needs, infection pressure and unsafe travel turn ordinary routines into meaningful risk decisions.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Zomboid

## Core principles for Haven

1. Every major threat has a warning period, visible preparation requirements and at least two viable counters.
2. A crisis affects population, happiness, utilities and buildings differently; it is not only a health-bar event.
3. Recovery costs time and money, but successful response grants relief funding and progression.
4. The city communicates its state visually through citizens, traffic, lighting, emergency colors and threat actors.
5. Random incidents are gated by city level, giving the player time to unlock the systems needed to answer them.

## Threat matrix

| Threat | Primary counters | Secondary counters | Main consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Flood | Drainage station | Parks, resilience | Building damage, population loss |
| Extreme UV | UV station | Reliable power | Population loss |
| Super storm | Upgraded buildings | Fire service, substations | Broad building damage |
| High waves | Seawalls | Marina, resilience | Coastal damage |
| Epidemic | Clinics and hospitals | Research institute | Population and happiness loss |
| Blackout | Substations | Spare solar capacity | Utility and service collapse |
| Zombie outbreak | Police and security hubs | Health care, shelters | Infection pressure and district damage |
| Giant monster | Defense towers | Research, police | Heavy infrastructure damage |

## Incident cadence

- The first automatic incident begins no earlier than day 4.
- Early cities receive climate and infrastructure incidents.
- Epidemics unlock at city level 2, zombie outbreaks at level 3, and giant monsters at level 4.
- Later incidents arrive every 3-5 days, with the interval affected by the number already survived.
- Emergency mobilization spends city funds to increase response strength before impact.

## Expansion path

- District evacuation routes and shelter capacity.
- Research tree for vaccines, drones, electromagnetic weapons and predictive sensors.
- Named citizen stories, injuries and rescue operations.
- Multi-stage boss threats that target power, water and government infrastructure.
- Difficulty directors with calm periods, tension building and crisis peaks.
